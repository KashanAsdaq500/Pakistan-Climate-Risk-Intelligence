# IPCC Sixth Assessment Report (AR6) — Regional Climate Assessment: South Asia & Pakistan

**Authoritative Source:** Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC), Working Groups I and II.  
**Official Reference:** IPCC AR6 Climate Change: The Physical Science Basis (Chapter 10 & 12 Regional Atlas for South Asia) and Impacts, Adaptation and Vulnerability.

## South Asia Observed Trends and Future Projections

The IPCC Sixth Assessment Report highlights critical trends specifically concerning the South Asian subcontinent and Pakistan:

1. **Observed Temperature Increases:**
   - South Asia has experienced statistically significant warming trends across the 20th and early 21st centuries. Mean and extreme temperatures have risen, with marked decreases in cold extremes and unprecedented increases in warm extremes.

2. **Intensification of Extreme Heatwaves:**
   - Extreme heatwaves will increase in frequency, duration, and geographic intensity under all greenhouse gas emissions scenarios (SSP1-2.6 through SSP5-8.5).
   - Humid heat stress is projected to approach or exceed dangerous thresholds for human survivability (wet-bulb temperatures \(T_w \ge 35^\circ\text{C}\)) in densely populated plains across the Indus and Ganges River basins.

3. **Compound Extremes and Hydrological Variability:**
   - In Pakistan, extreme temperature spikes during May-June often directly precede erratic monsoon precipitation, increasing risks of compound disasters (heatwaves followed by catastrophic flash floods and riverine flooding, as observed in 2022).
   - Accelerated glacier melt in the Hindu Kush-Himalaya-Karakoram (HKH) range alters seasonal water availability and increases Glacier Lake Outburst Flood (GLOF) risks.

## Difference Between Weather and Climate

- **Weather:** Describes the atmospheric conditions at a specific place and time over short intervals (minutes, hours, days). Parameters include real-time temperature, humidity, rainfall, wind speed, and cloud cover.
- **Climate:** Represents the long-term statistical pattern and distribution of weather over extended periods (standard climatological reference period is 30 years as defined by the WMO).
- **Climate Risk Assessment:** Uses historical observations and mathematical modeling to assess the probability and severity of adverse climate events under varying atmospheric and environmental scenarios.

## Interpreting Temperature-Based Heat Risk

When analyzing model estimates for maximum temperature:
- **LOW Risk (< 35°C):** Ambient temperatures within baseline tolerance for healthy individuals; normal hydration adequate.
- **MODERATE Risk (35.0°C – 39.9°C):** Heat discomfort heightened; increased risk of fatigue and heat cramps during prolonged physical exertion.
- **HIGH Risk (40.0°C – 44.9°C):** Dangerous thermal load; rapid onset of heat exhaustion likely with outdoor activity; vulnerable populations require immediate cooling.
- **EXTREME Risk (>= 45.0°C):** Life-threatening environmental heat; risk of heatstroke without active shelter, air conditioning, and physiological cooling.
