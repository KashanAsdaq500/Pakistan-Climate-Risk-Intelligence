# NASA Earth Observatory — Urban Heat Islands and Remote Sensing of Surface Temperatures

**Authoritative Source:** National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA), Earth Science Division & Earth Observatory.  
**Official Reference:** NASA Earth Observatory Science Features on Urban Heat Islands, MODIS Land Surface Temperature (LST), and ECOSTRESS Thermal Infrared Radiometer.

## What is an Urban Heat Island (UHI)?

An Urban Heat Island occurs when urbanized regions experience substantially higher temperatures than surrounding rural or vegetated areas. NASA satellite thermal observations identify key mechanisms behind this phenomenon:

1. **Thermal Mass and Low-Albedo Surfaces:**
   - Concrete, asphalt, brick, and dark roofing materials possess low solar reflectivity (albedo) and high thermal capacity. They absorb incoming solar radiation during peak daylight and reradiate heat long into the night.
2. **Loss of Evapotranspirative Cooling:**
   - Natural vegetation, soil, and canopy cover dissipate sensible heat through evapotranspiration. When vegetation is cleared for impervious concrete structures, this primary evaporative cooling mechanism is lost.
3. **Anthropogenic Heat Emissions:**
   - Vehicles, heavy industry, electrical generators, and air-conditioning units reject concentrated heat directly into the localized street-level atmospheric canopy.
4. **Urban Canyon Trapping:**
   - Tall buildings create urban canyons that reduce wind circulation and repeatedly trap reflected and radiated thermal energy.

## Land Surface Temperature (LST) vs. Air Temperature

NASA instruments like the Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer (MODIS) and ECOSTRESS measure **Land Surface Temperature (LST)**:
- **Surface Temperature:** Measures how hot the direct physical surface of the ground, pavement, or building skin feels to the touch. Pavement temperatures during hot summer days can reach 15°C to 25°C higher than ambient air.
- **Air Temperature:** Standard meteorological air temperature measured in shaded Stevenson screens at 1.5 to 2 meters above ground level.

## Climate Adaptation and Urban Heat Island Mitigation

NASA research and urban climatology demonstrate effective countermeasures:
- **Cool Roofs and High-Albedo Pavements:** Applying reflective white coatings or high-albedo materials reduces surface absorption of incoming solar radiation.
- **Urban Forestry and Green Corridors:** Introducing urban tree canopies and green spaces creates microclimate cooling through shading and transpiration.
- **Water Bodies and Microclimate Design:** Incorporating urban water features and shaded breezeways facilitates convective heat dissipation.
