# Pakistan Meteorological Department (PMD) — Heatwave Criteria and Climatology

**Authoritative Source:** Pakistan Meteorological Department (PMD), Ministry of Climate Change and Environmental Coordination, Government of Pakistan.  
**Official Reference:** PMD National Heatwave Early Warning Center Bulletins & Technical Heatwave Definitions.

## Heatwave Definitions in Pakistan

According to the Pakistan Meteorological Department (PMD), a heatwave condition is declared based on the following meteorological thresholds:

1. **Plains of Punjab, Sindh, and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa:**
   - A heatwave is identified when the maximum temperature remains **4.0°C to 5.0°C above normal** climatological temperatures for a continuous period of at least three days.
   - A **severe heatwave** is declared when the maximum temperature persists at **6.0°C or higher above normal** for at least three consecutive days.
   - Absolute threshold: Daytime temperatures consistently reaching or exceeding **45.0°C** in southern and central plains represent critical thermal risk.

2. **Coastal Regions (Karachi and Makran Coast):**
   - Coastal heatwaves in Karachi and southern Sindh are governed by both temperature and humidity (heat index). PMD criteria consider sustained maximum temperatures above **40.0°C** combined with westerly to south-westerly maritime breeze stoppage, high relative humidity, and dry north/northwesterly continental winds from Balochistan.

## Climatological Heatwave Seasons in Pakistan

In Pakistan, heatwaves predominantly manifest in two distinct windows:
- **Pre-Monsoon Peak (May to June):** The most intense, prolonged heatwaves occur from mid-May through June before the onset of the South Asian Summer Monsoon. Central and upper Sindh (Jacobabad, Sibi, Larkana, Mohenjo-daro) and Southern Punjab (Bahawalpur, Multan, Rahim Yar Khan) routinely observe daytime temperatures crossing 46°C to 50°C.
- **Post-Monsoon Heat Spell (September to October):** Following the retreat of monsoon clouds, secondary temperature spikes occur, particularly in coastal and low-altitude zones.

## Historical Temperature Benchmarks in Pakistan

- **Jacobabad & Mohenjo-daro:** Recognized globally among the hottest inhabited locations on Earth, having recorded historical maximums reaching up to **52°C to 53.5°C** (e.g., Mohenjo-daro on May 26, 2010 reached 53.5°C).
- **Turbat (Balochistan):** Recorded 53.5°C on May 28, 2017, matching the Asian all-time highest temperature benchmark verified by the World Meteorological Organization (WMO).
- **Karachi Heatwave (June 2015):** A catastrophic low-pressure system in the Arabian Sea cut off the cooling sea breeze while dry continental winds drove temperatures to 44.8°C with high humidity, resulting in extensive heat-related morbidity and mortality.

## Role of PMD Early Warnings

PMD operates specialized Regional Meteorological Centres and issues 3-to-5-day advance Heatwave Alerts to provincial disaster management authorities (PDMA), civil administrations, and public health systems to trigger local municipal response plans.
