# World Health Organization (WHO) — Heatwaves and Health: Guidance on Prevention and Care

**Authoritative Source:** World Health Organization (WHO) / World Meteorological Organization (WMO) Joint Office for Climate and Health.  
**Official Reference:** WHO Public Health Advice on Preventing Health Impacts of Heat; Heat and Health Information Series.

## Physiological Impact of Extreme Heat on the Human Body

The human body maintains internal core temperature within a narrow physiological range of approximately 36.5°C to 37.5°C. Excessive environmental temperature stresses the body's thermoregulatory mechanisms:

1. **Thermoregulation Breakdown:** When ambient air temperature exceeds core body temperature (approx. 37°C), the body cannot lose heat by radiation or convection and relies exclusively on evaporation through sweat.
2. **Humid Heat Stress:** If high ambient temperatures coincide with elevated relative humidity, sweat cannot evaporate effectively, leading to rapid core temperature escalation.
3. **Cardiovascular Overload:** Peripheral vasodilation directs massive blood flow to the skin to dissipate heat, requiring dramatically elevated heart rates and cardiac output. For individuals with underlying cardiac weakness, this can precipitate acute myocardial infarction or cardiac arrest.
4. **Fluid and Electrolyte Depletion:** Heavy perspiration causes hypovolemia, loss of essential sodium and potassium, acute renal failure, and hemoconcentration that increases thrombosis risk.

## Clinical Spectrum of Heat-Related Illnesses

- **Heat Cramps:** Painful muscle contractions primarily in the legs and abdomen caused by rapid salt loss during heavy physical exertion in hot settings.
- **Heat Exhaustion:** Characterized by heavy sweating, pale or clammy skin, rapid weak pulse, dizziness, nausea, headache, fainting, and moderate core temperature elevation (under 40°C).
- **Heatstroke (Medical Emergency):** A life-threatening condition where core body temperature exceeds **40.0°C (104°F)**. Hallmarked by central nervous system dysfunction: confusion, delirium, slurred speech, seizures, loss of consciousness, and hot, dry or profuse sweating skin. Heatstroke requires emergency hospitalization and immediate active cooling.

## Heat-Health Precaution Guidelines

WHO recommends the following core measures:
- **Keep Your Home Cool:** Close windows and shutters during daytime hot hours; open them late in the evening and early morning if outside air is cooler.
- **Keep Out of the Heat:** Move to the coolest room in the house, particularly at night. If indoor temperatures exceed 32°C, utilize fans with caution (electric fans above 35°C can exacerbate dehydration unless skin or clothing is wetted).
- **Hydration:** Consume fluids regularly, avoiding alcohol, excessive caffeine, and excessively high sugar intake.
- **Immediate First-Aid for Heat Illness:** Move affected individuals to a cool, shaded environment, loosen clothing, apply cool damp cloths or ice to neck, armpits, and groin, fan aggressively, and call medical emergency services.
